The current example uses Rust's `bayesian_metropolis_chains` operation through
[execute](/innovate/api/generated/execute/). It replaces the earlier
`NumpyroFitter` tutorial. Its purpose is to check a sampler against a known
answer before considering a less tractable scientific model.

## Learning outcomes

You will derive the target mean and variance for one bounded parameter, run four
chains from dispersed starts, and distinguish stored draws from effective sample
size. You will explain why good diagnostic values and agreement in one fixture
do not prove convergence or validate an empirical likelihood.

## Prerequisites

Use the [native development installation](/innovate/user-guide/installation/).
The example needs the installed package and Python's standard library, with no
external dataset or network access. Basic familiarity with a Normal likelihood,
a Normal prior and variance is helpful.

Only the Logistic asymptote `L` is unknown. The slope `k = 0.5` and midpoint
`x0 = 2` are fixed. The single synthetic observation is `y = 50` at `t = 2`,
where the model gives `y = L / 2`. The observation noise standard deviation is
one and the prior for `L` has mean 96 and standard deviation two. Support is
restricted to `[80, 120]`.

## Interpretation

At the midpoint, observing 50 with noise variance one supplies a likelihood
for `L` centered at 100 with variance four. The prior also has variance four,
centered at 96. Adding their precisions gives posterior variance
`1 / (1/4 + 1/4) = 2`; the precision-weighted posterior mean is 98. The finite
support truncates this Normal distribution. Both boundaries are more than
12 posterior standard deviations from the mean, so truncation is negligible at
the declared tolerances, though it is not mathematically absent.

The script allows absolute deviations below 0.2 for the sample mean and 0.3 for
the sample variance. It also checks rank-normalized split R-hat below 1.05 and
bulk and tail effective sample sizes above 1000. These are fixture regression
bounds, not an empirical analysis acceptance policy. The diagnostic framework
and its limits are discussed by
[Vehtari and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.1214/20-BA1221), with
[the authors' code and supplementary examples](https://github.com/avehtari/rhat_ess).
For a new analysis,
[Section 2 of the author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/1903.08008v5#S2)
recommends at least four chains and `R-hat < 1.01` as a first-level check.
Assess Monte Carlo error for the particular quantities and precision you need.

**Reader task:** why is the posterior mean 98 rather than the likelihood's 100?
The prior and likelihood have equal precision in this case, so the mean is their
average. If the prior standard deviation were changed, the independent target
calculation and tolerances would need deliberate review before changing the
checked fixture.

## Limitations

One observation cannot identify `L`, `k` and `x0` jointly in this example; the
last two are deliberately fixed. The case does not test a hierarchical model,
multimodal posterior, empirical prior, calibrated credible-interval coverage or
an appropriate observation model for real adoption counts.

A favorable R-hat or effective sample size is a diagnostic observation, not proof
that all posterior regions were explored. The posterior oracle strengthens this
specific test but does not establish general sampler adequacy. Use the
[native capability guide](/innovate/native-migration/) for supported Bayesian
operations and the scientific qualification work that remains open.
