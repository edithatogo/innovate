This lesson fits two descriptive curves to Australian Internet-use observations
from the International Telecommunication Union (ITU), distributed through the
World Bank's World Development Indicators. It takes you from a frozen public
dataset through native fitting, prediction and a comparison with a persistence
baseline. The example runs offline after installation.

## Learning outcomes

You will define the observed quantity and its units, check a dataset's identity,
retain real calendar time when observations are missing, fit two models using
only a training period, and score predictions on later years. You will also
explain why a converged fit can perform worse than a simple baseline.

## Prerequisites

Use the [development installation](/innovate/user-guide/installation/) with the
supported native extension and Polars. Read the
[first prediction example](/innovate/user-guide/getting-started/) if the table
interface is new to you. No account or external data service is needed to run
this lesson: its attributed extract is committed with the example.

**Observation definition:** `IT.NET.USER.ZS` is the annual percentage of people
who used the Internet in the preceding three months. It measures recent use,
not cumulative first adoption or a count of new adopters. The
[World Bank metadata](https://databank.worldbank.org/metadataglossary/world-development-indicators/series/IT.NET.USER.ZS)
identifies ITU as the source and specifies CC BY 4.0. Attribute ITU and World Bank
when reusing the extract; its manifest includes the licence link.

## Steps

<Steps>

1. **Inspect the frozen extract.** The directory
   `docs/datasets/wdi-aus-internet-20261116` contains the original API response,
   a derived CSV and a manifest with source URL, retrieval date, revision,
   attribution, transformation and SHA-256 hashes. The response contains 36
   calendar years from 1990 through 2025; 32 have observations.

2. **Preserve time and missingness.** The CSV keeps 2002–2004 and 2025 missing.
   Fitting excludes those rows, while `time = year - 1990` retains the elapsed
   years. Missing use is not zero use. The example never sums percentages,
   interpolates observations or compresses the calendar.

3. **Run the checked comparison from the repository root.** Training ends in
   2015. The example fits native Logistic and Gompertz models to 23 observations,
   then predicts the nine observed years from 2016 through 2024. The persistence
   baseline predicts the final training observation for every holdout year.

   ```sh
   uv run --locked python examples/native_public_data.py
   ```

4. **Inspect diagnostics and later-year errors.** The script requires successful
   numerical termination and checks preserved year identity and finite outputs.
   It calculates MAE, RMSE and signed prediction bias directly from held-out
   residuals. These errors are in percentage points, not relative percentages.
   No test observation is supplied to either fit or the baseline.

</Steps>

## Interpretation

This is a **retrospective calendar holdout using the November 2026 data
vintage**. It is not an as-of-2015 real-time backtest: historical observations
may have been revised after that date. The frozen extract makes this particular
exercise reproducible, while a real forecasting study also needs the data
vintage that would have been available at each forecast origin.

The curves estimate a descriptive saturation shape. They do not identify why
Internet use changed or establish a causal diffusion mechanism. In this split,
the fitted curves settle below later observations. A high training fit statistic
or local numerical convergence would therefore be a poor substitute for the
later-year comparison.

**Reader exercise:** inspect the notebook's predictions, then change the cutoff
to 2018. Which model's error changes most? Explain why choosing a cutoff or model
after viewing these results makes the exercise exploratory. A final assessment
needs an untouched evaluation period or a prespecified rolling-origin design.

## Limitations

One country, one indicator and one historical split do not establish broad model
performance. Survey definitions, reporting differences and revisions can affect
the observations. This lesson uses no measurement-error model, observation
weights or prediction intervals; convergence does not establish identifiability,
global optimality or calibrated uncertainty. A fitted asymptote is an estimate,
not an externally established population ceiling.

The manifest's checksums detect changed files against the recorded extract.
They do not authenticate a provider, prove the licence independently or freeze
the upstream service. New data should receive a separately reviewed snapshot,
with the changes and transformation recorded. Continue with
[dataset integrity](/innovate/tutorials/data-ingestion-provenance/) or the
[model atlas](/innovate/models/) to choose another supported task.
