This tutorial constructs a known pause in a stochastic Bass adoption process.
A piecewise-constant covariate cancels innovation and imitation until time three;
a later event increases innovation at time six. You will compare this schedule
with an equivalent sequence of additive events and inspect the tracked agents.
The controls are supplied assumptions in a synthetic experiment.

## Learning outcomes

You will distinguish observation times from control-change times, calculate the
effective rates in each interval, and verify that a zero-rate interval produces
no adoptions. You will also check replay, agent conservation and rejection of
negative rates or control changes that collide at the selected tick resolution.

## Prerequisites

Install the [native development environment](/innovate/user-guide/installation/)
and read [Simulate a seeded Bass adoption path](/innovate/tutorials/kairos-simulation/).
Use the same checkout and installed native package. No external data or service
is needed.

The scientific model key is `bass`; `kairos_abm` selects a native execution
engine that also records homogeneous, exchangeable agent identities. It does
not introduce network ties or fitted individual effects. This example has
100 agents and a market level `m=100`, so its count and level happen to have the
same numerical value.

Use one consistent numeric time unit. The baseline `p=0.2` and `q=0.3` have
inverse-time units. Here the covariate `x` is dimensionless, so `beta_p`,
`beta_q`, `delta_p` and `delta_q` also have inverse-time units. The observation
grid is `[0, 1, 3, 5, 6, 10]`; its unequal gaps do not imply pauses in execution.

## Interpretation

The covariate is `-1` from time zero until three, then zero. Its coefficients
equal the baseline rates, so the effective schedule is:

| Time interval | Covariate contribution | Accumulated event contribution | Effective `p`, `q` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 up to 3 | `-0.2`, `-0.3` | `0`, `0` | `0`, `0` |
| 3 up to 6 | `0`, `0` | `0`, `0` | `0.2`, `0.3` |
| 6 onward | `0`, `0` | `0.1`, `0` | `0.3`, `0.3` |

The schedule is right-continuous: the new coefficients apply at their change
time. A coefficient change does not itself create an adopter. The
[simulation clock](https://github.com/edithatogo/innovate/blob/main/rust/crates/innovate-core/src/simulation.rs)
rounds a sampled waiting time upward to at least one tick, and a control
boundary precedes an adoption event tied to that boundary. Consequently the
restored rates at time three still leave the count at that exact boundary zero.

`ticks_per_unit=1_000_000` gives a tick duration of one millionth of the chosen
time unit. Change times are mapped with `ceil(time * ticks_per_unit)`;
observations sample completed events through `floor(time * ticks_per_unit)`.
The fixture uses integer change and observation times, which are exactly on
this tick grid. It does not require observations at every tick or every event.
Between recorded times, the process continues under the active coefficients.

### Why the alternative schedule agrees

The event-only specification subtracts `0.2` and `0.3` at time zero, adds them
back at time three, and adds another `0.1` to innovation at time six. This gives
the same boundaries and effective rates as the covariate specification. With
the same seed and engine, the fixture verifies matching counts, levels and
agent assignments. This is a controlled equivalence check for these schedules;
it is not a general claim that different interventions share identical random
paths.

The negative tests exercise two different mistakes. Setting `beta_p=2` while
`x=-1` makes innovation `0.2-2=-1.8`, which is rejected instead of clamped to
zero. At one tick per unit, distinct events at `0.1` and `0.2` both map to tick
one; the native contract rejects that collision instead of silently merging
the changes.

## Limitations

This is one seeded synthetic path with homogeneous agents. The covariate is a
specified additive control, not a fitted regression effect. The pause and
equivalence assertions do not validate an empirical intervention, identify
causal effects or establish uncertainty coverage. A positive rate also does
not guarantee that every agent adopts before a finite horizon.

Replay is checked for the same request and installed runtime. Changing time
resolution, engine, initial conditions, seed or implementation revision can
change the path. Preserve these details with the result. For graph-mediated
transmission and service queues, continue with
[network demand and completed adoption](/innovate/tutorials/network-scenarios/);
those mechanisms have different exposure and completion contracts.
